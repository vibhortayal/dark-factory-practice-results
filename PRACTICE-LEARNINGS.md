### R1 rule audit and rule-clean rehearsal protocol (2026-09-28)

Source: pinned official [participant guide](https://github.com/band-ai/dark-factory-wearedevs/blob/803560d2a678ace1414465c098eb0ab5380ffade/docs/participant-guide.md), "Eligibility," "Agent Teamwork," and "Build and check each stage." The guide allows human intervention in *development* runs but requires a fresh room/repo for the submitted run. From a stage's task dispatch until the coordinator's final report, that dispatch is its only human input: no steering, debugging hints, approval, confirmation, clarification, extra decision or same-stage rerun. Seats resolve choices together; unresolved blockers become the stage outcome. No "looks good, continue" between stage dispatches. A roster of **at least three distinct configured coding-agent seats** is required, and at least two seats must exchange reciprocal literal `@handle` messages. The guide gives no upper seat limit or per-stage wall-clock limit; submission closes October 5, 2026, 23:59 PDT. It does not expressly name Instinct, but an operator/assistant correction to seats is additional outside input and so is not rule-clean.

**R1 interventions that could not happen during a submitted stage:** operator correction `b56bca1e` telling Verifier to reject Architect's altered paraphrase and use canonical dispatch; `f5a25ad4` identifying the Implementer commit/checkout for Architect; `44e98088` telling Verifier how to characterize findings; `19cad68c` giving Implementer a focused defect list and repair instructions after the inconclusive Verifier outcome. The operator also sequenced/stopped/restarted seats and repaired the environment mid-stage (Node/Docker installation and Claude credential/spend setup); these are development operations and would be outside steering if they affect execution after dispatch in a graded-format run. Initial dispatch and independently verified post-outcome evaluation are allowed at their proper boundaries. Seat-to-seat autonomous critiques and fixes are allowed; external mid-stage prompts are not. The local operator merge of `ce2be42` was repository preparation, not proof that the band completed a full autonomous stage or that the remote repo was delivered. R1 remains a development run, not a graded-format autonomous rehearsal; its code and room are kept unaltered as evidence.

**For the one full-fidelity dress rehearsal, then the submitted run:** preflight the fresh room, repo, exact spec/pin, three or more seats, generic mandates, routing, cloned checkout, credentials, billing cap, Docker/Node/harness, and runtime tests *before* stage dispatch. In those zero-intervention runs, the coordinator's dispatched task is the last outside input until its final stage report. Watch read-only while seats work. Do not post in-room corrections, prompts, approval, debugging hints, repair requests, or "continue" messages; do not rescue a failed handoff or revise a seat's private todo mid-run. If a blocker or false PASS appears, record it as the run outcome, audit independently afterward, and change the generic protocol/mandates or environment BETWEEN runs. Use R1 as development, and R2 as development only if defects remain; then one fresh-room/fresh-repo zero-intervention dress rehearsal before the actual graded run, preserving all earlier rooms and repos. Development runs may use bounded interventions to fix defects, but label them development and keep token spend lean. Do not call a later operator-repaired stage a clean autonomous run. The actual submitted run must still satisfy all official gates and use the official harness; a rehearsal's PASS never substitutes for that evidence.
